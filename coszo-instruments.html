COSZO Instruments

The new sensor suite that COSZO is adding to the Regional Cabled Array, designed specifically for subduction zone geophysics and earthquake early warning.

COSZO is adding a geophysical sensor suite purpose-built for subduction zone science. The instruments below produce the COSZO data products, spanning seismic, pressure, acoustic, and oceanographic observations.

Science Junction Box

Designed & built by: UW Applied Physics Laboratory, Electronic & Photonic Systems Department.

Science junction boxes connect to the Regional Cabled Array nodes via 200-meter cables and are equipped with wet-mateable ports that provide low-voltage power and real-time internet connectivity to the scientific instruments. New junction boxes will be installed at the Outer Shelf (PN1D), Oregon Offshore (PN1C), and Mid Slope (PN1B) nodes; at Oregon Shelf the instruments join a preexisting junction box (MJ01C). Each new junction box also has 2–3 unused ports available for additional instruments through PI-driven initiatives.

Sites: Outer Shelf, Oregon Offshore, Mid Slope.

Broadband Seismometer & Strong Motion

Make & model: Nanometrics Atlantis Cabled Observatory (T360-COBST2).

Three-component broadband seismometers paired with high-dynamic-range strong-motion accelerometers. The broadband channels capture regional and teleseismic ground velocity for ambient-noise interferometry and moment-tensor work, while the co-located strong-motion sensors record the unclipped, near-source acceleration of a great earthquake.

Each ocean bottom seismic (OBS) package houses a Trillium 360 ocean bottom broadband seismometer, a Class A Titan accelerometer, and a Centaur Gen5 datalogger within a titanium pressure vessel proven for deployment depths up to 6,000 m. It has a dynamic range of 220 dB at 1 Hz, a 4 g acceleration clip level, and a noise floor below the Peterson Low Noise Model (Peterson, 1993) from 200 s to 10 Hz. Each package is placed within a caisson, buried in the sediment, and covered in silica beads; a low-frequency hydrophone and a differential pressure gauge connect directly to it via 5 m cables.

Sites: Oregon Shelf, Outer Shelf, Oregon Offshore, Mid Slope.

Differential Pressure Gauge

Design: Deep-sea differential pressure gauge (Cox et al., 1984).

Measures short-period pressure variations at the seafloor, capturing tsunami signals and hydroacoustic waves that complement the absolute pressure record. Collocated with the broadband seismometer, it helps distinguish seismic signals such as tremor from acoustic arrivals and enables optimal corrections for tilt noise on vertical seismometer channels.

The DPG is depth-rated for 6,000 m with a passband of 2 Hz to 500 s and noise of 5 × 10-3 Pa²/Hz at 100 s. It is mounted on a tripod about one meter above the seafloor and connected to the OBS package with a 5 m cable.

Sites: Oregon Shelf, Outer Shelf, Oregon Offshore, Mid Slope.

Hydrophone

Make & model: HTI-90-U low-frequency hydrophone.

Low-frequency hydrophones tuned to the infrasound band for T-phase detection, hydroacoustic event characterization, and long-range propagation studies.

The HTI-90-U has a frequency response ranging from 2 Hz to 20 kHz, a maximum operating depth of 6,096 m, and a self-noise of 63 dB re 1 µPa. It is mounted on a tripod about one meter above the seafloor and connected to the OBS package with a 5 m cable.

Sites: Oregon Shelf, Outer Shelf, Oregon Offshore, Mid Slope.

Absolute Pressure Gauge

Make & model: Paroscientific Series 8000 Submersible Depth Sensor.

Absolute seafloor pressure sensors recording the tidal band, with a paired temperature channel on each instrument. These feed the Absolute Seafloor Pressure data product for tsunami detection and long-period seafloor deformation monitoring. The high-resolution, uncalibrated time series also help identify pressure-calibration artifacts that could be confused with slow slip, and support compliance measurements sensitive to changes in seismic velocity structure.

The Series 8000 incorporates Digiquartz transducers into submersible housings as depth sensors; the pressure range of each instrument is calibrated to its deployment depth to maximize performance.

GSSM — Calibrated Pressure & Acceleration

Designed & built by: UW Applied Physics Laboratory (Wilcock et al., 2021).

The Geodetic and Seismic Sensor Module combines calibrated pressure and strong-motion platforms, providing both geodetic and seismological observations from the same seafloor site. Its calibrated pressure time series can be used to measure secular vertical strain and detect slow slip events.

Each GSSM utilizes a Paroscientific Seismic Oceanographic Sensor module (Model 7116-2K-2 for 1,400 m depth; Model 7116-1K-2 for 700 m depth) comprising two Paroscientific Digiquartz pressure transducers (Model 44.4K-302; maximum depth rating of 1,400 m, range of 0–4,400 psi, and accuracy of 0.01% of full scale with parts-per-billion resolution possible), a Digiquartz nano-resolution barometer (Model 6000-16B), and a Quartz Seismic Sensor 1.5 g accelerometer (Model QA-15). It employs the A-0-A method: a VICI HPLC valve periodically switches the two pressure transducers from measuring external water pressure to measuring the internal pressure of the titanium housing for calibration, and an internal temperature sensor measures and corrects for thermal effects.

Sites: Outer Shelf, Oregon Offshore, Mid Slope.

CSCPR — Calibrated Pressure

Designed & built by: UC San Diego Scripps Institution of Oceanography (Sasagawa & Zumberge, 2013).

A cabled self-calibrating pressure recorder with on-board calibration against a known reference, correcting the long-period instrumental drift that normally obscures tectonic deformation signals. Its calibrated measurements duplicate those of the GSSM, providing a long-term check on the validity of the GSSM calibrations — difficult to do in the laboratory because of the challenges of maintaining stable, high pressures.

The instrument includes two redundant Paroscientific quartz pressure gauges (Model 42 K) that are periodically switched between the ocean pressure signal and a stable, reproducible reference pressure generated by an onboard DH Instruments piston gauge (Model PC-7300-2), using motor-driven Swagelok valves (Series 41G) turned by Hanbay motorized actuators (Model MDM1000). The sensors measure the shift in the resonant frequency of a quartz force transducer attached to a Bourdon tube as the applied pressure changes, with a sensitivity of order 0.005 kPa. The instruments and electronics are housed in a titanium housing, which also hosts a temperature sensor.

Sites: Oregon Offshore, Mid Slope.

Current Meter

Make & model: Nortek Vector.

Measures near-bottom sea-water velocity, providing the oceanographic context needed to model and remove current- and tide-driven pressure fluctuations before a tectonic signal becomes visible, and contributing to an understanding of seismic noise that might be confused with tremor.

The Nortek Vector single-point current meter acquires 3D measurements at rates up to 64 Hz and is suitable for deployment down to a depth of 4,000 m. The instrument sits one meter above the seafloor.

Sites: Outer Shelf, Oregon Offshore, Mid Slope.

COSZO

The Cascadia Offshore Subduction Zone Observatory is an NSF Mid-scale Research Infrastructure project adding geophysical instrumentation to the OOI Regional Cabled Array off the coast of Oregon to understand the science of subduction-zone hazard.
